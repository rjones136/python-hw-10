The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. Frequency analysis is one of the oldest tools in code breaking. Long before computers, Arab scholars counted how often each letter appeared in ordinary writing, and they used those counts to break secret messages. In English, the letter E appears more often than any other letter. The letters T, A, O, I, N, S, H, and R are also very common, while J, Q, X, and Z are rare. A secret message written with a simple substitution cipher keeps those same patterns, so counting letters can reveal the code. The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog again and again, and the lazy dog sleeps in the warm afternoon sun.
